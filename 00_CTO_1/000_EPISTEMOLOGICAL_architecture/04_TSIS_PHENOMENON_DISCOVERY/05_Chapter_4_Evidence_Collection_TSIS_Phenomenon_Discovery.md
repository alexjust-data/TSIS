# 4. Evidence Collection

## 4.1 Purpose

A hypothesis becomes scientifically meaningful only after evidence has
been collected under controlled conditions.

The purpose of this chapter is to define how TSIS gathers evidence
without contaminating the research process.

Evidence is not created.

Evidence is observed.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4.2 Fundamental Principle

Evidence must always be collected before interpretation.

``` text
Observation
    ↓
Representation
    ↓
Evidence
    ↓
Interpretation
```

Never:

``` text
Interpretation
    ↓
Evidence
```

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4.3 Definition

Evidence is a reproducible collection of observable states supporting or
contradicting a research hypothesis.

Evidence is population-based.

Single examples are illustrations, not evidence.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4.4 Evidence sources

Evidence may originate from:

``` text
Market States
Event States
Microstructure Windows
Scanner Candidates
Matched Controls
Historical Context
Execution Context
```

Every source shall be explicitly documented.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4.5 Evidence populations

Each evidence collection process shall define:

``` text
Target Population
Control Population
Sampling Rules
Eligibility Rules
Observation Horizon
```

No evidence exists independently of its population.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4.6 Positive and negative evidence

TSIS collects both:

``` text
Supporting Evidence

Contradictory Evidence
```

Contradictory observations are preserved rather than discarded.

Scientific progress depends on both.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4.7 Control populations

Every phenomenon investigation should include appropriate controls.

Examples:

``` text
Matched Non-Events
Near Misses
Random Eligible States
Matched Time-of-Day Samples
Matched Liquidity Samples
```

Controls reduce selection bias.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4.8 Evidence quality

Evidence quality depends on:

-   coverage,
-   representativeness,
-   temporal legality,
-   reproducibility,
-   data quality,
-   sample integrity.

Quality must be measured before conclusions are drawn.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4.9 Evidence metadata

Every evidence collection shall declare:

``` text
evidence_id
hypothesis_id
population_id
control_population_id
representation_versions
state_version
collection_protocol
sample_size
quality_state
```

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4.10 Evidence repository

Evidence should be preserved as a permanent research asset.

Future hypotheses may reuse historical evidence provided that provenance
remains intact.

Evidence is therefore cumulative.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4.11 Constitutional rule

Evidence shall always be collected using predefined populations,
explicit eligibility rules and reproducible procedures.

No phenomenon may be declared without documented evidence gathered
according to these principles.
