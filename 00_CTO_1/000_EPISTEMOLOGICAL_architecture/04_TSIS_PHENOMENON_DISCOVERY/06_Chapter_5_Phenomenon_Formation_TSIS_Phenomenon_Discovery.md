# 5. Phenomenon Formation

## 5.1 Purpose

This chapter defines how empirical evidence becomes a scientific market
phenomenon inside TSIS.

A phenomenon is **not** a feature, a state or a strategy.

A phenomenon is a validated explanation of recurring observable
behaviour.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 5.2 Fundamental Principle

A phenomenon cannot be declared.

It must emerge from reproducible evidence.

``` text
Research Question
        ↓
Hypothesis
        ↓
Evidence
        ↓
Validation
        ↓
Phenomenon Candidate
        ↓
Independent Confirmation
        ↓
Canonical Phenomenon
```

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 5.3 Definition

A market phenomenon is a reproducible behavioural pattern that:

-   emerges across a defined population,
-   is supported by empirical evidence,
-   survives validation,
-   remains independent of any specific strategy.

Phenomena describe markets.

Strategies exploit phenomena.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 5.4 Necessary conditions

Every phenomenon shall satisfy:

-   observable foundation,
-   explicit hypothesis,
-   declared research population,
-   matched controls,
-   reproducible evidence,
-   temporal legality,
-   documented limitations.

Failure of any condition prevents promotion.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 5.5 Population specificity

A phenomenon is always conditional upon its validated population.

Example:

``` text
Liquidity Vacuum
```

validated for:

``` text
Low-float halt resumptions
```

does not automatically generalize to:

``` text
Large-cap index constituents
```

Generalization requires independent evidence.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 5.6 Competing explanations

Multiple hypotheses may explain the same observations.

TSIS preserves competing explanations until evidence clearly
discriminates among them.

Architecture remains hypothesis-neutral.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 5.7 Phenomenon metadata

Every phenomenon shall declare:

``` text
phenomenon_id
canonical_name
research_question_id
validated_population
control_population
supporting_hypotheses
evidence_version
state_version
feature_registry_version
validation_protocol
status
```

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 5.8 Status lifecycle

Suggested statuses:

``` text
Candidate
Supported
Replicated
Canonical
Under Review
Deprecated
```

Canonical status requires independent replication.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 5.9 Relationship with strategies

Phenomena never prescribe actions.

Instead:

``` text
Phenomenon
        ↓
Knowledge
        ↓
Possible Strategy
```

Multiple strategies may exploit the same phenomenon.

One strategy may depend on several phenomena.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 5.10 Constitutional rule

A canonical TSIS phenomenon is a scientific knowledge object.

It shall never be promoted solely because it improves historical
profitability.

Promotion requires reproducible empirical evidence across the declared
research population.
