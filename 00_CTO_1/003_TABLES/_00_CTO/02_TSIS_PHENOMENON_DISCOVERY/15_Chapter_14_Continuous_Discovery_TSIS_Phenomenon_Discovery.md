# 14. Continuous Discovery

## 14.1 Purpose

Scientific discovery inside TSIS is not a sequence of isolated projects.

It is a continuous, self-improving process in which every completed
investigation expands the future search space.

The objective of this chapter is to define how TSIS continuously
generates, prioritizes and revisits research opportunities.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 14.2 Fundamental Principle

Discovery never stops.

Every validated result, rejected hypothesis and newly available dataset
may generate new research questions.

``` text
Knowledge
      ↓
New Questions
      ↓
New Research
      ↓
New Knowledge
```

Knowledge therefore acts as the seed of future discovery.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 14.3 Discovery Loop

The canonical discovery cycle is:

``` text
Observations
        ↓
Representations
        ↓
Research Questions
        ↓
Hypotheses
        ↓
Evidence
        ↓
Knowledge
        ↓
New Questions
```

The process is recursive rather than linear.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 14.4 Sources of new questions

New investigations may originate from:

-   newly discovered phenomena,
-   contradictory evidence,
-   regime changes,
-   new feature families,
-   new data sources,
-   model failures,
-   execution failures,
-   unexplained residual behaviour.

Research opportunities should be explicitly catalogued.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 14.5 Discovery backlog

Every open research opportunity should become a governed backlog item.

Minimum metadata:

``` text
research_opportunity_id
origin
related_knowledge
priority
expected_value
estimated_complexity
required_datasets
status
```

The backlog becomes the roadmap for scientific evolution.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 14.6 Revisiting knowledge

Canonical knowledge is periodically re-evaluated when:

-   new evidence appears,
-   larger populations become available,
-   new representations are introduced,
-   contradictory evidence accumulates.

Re-evaluation is expected, not exceptional.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 14.7 Automation

Future autonomous research systems may propose:

-   new hypotheses,
-   new feature families,
-   new populations,
-   new validation protocols.

Human review remains responsible for canonical promotion.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 14.8 Scientific debt

Unanswered questions, unresolved contradictions and partially validated
hypotheses constitute scientific debt.

TSIS tracks this debt explicitly instead of allowing it to disappear
into documentation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 14.9 Discovery metrics

Suggested metrics include:

``` text
questions_created
questions_closed
validated_knowledge
replication_rate
contradictory_findings
knowledge_revisions
research_cycle_time
```

Metrics evaluate the research process, not trading profitability.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 14.10 Constitutional rule

TSIS is designed as a permanently evolving scientific laboratory.

The completion of one investigation is never considered the end of
discovery; it is the beginning of the next cycle of knowledge
generation.
