# 1. Philosophy of Scientific Discovery

## 1.1 Purpose

This chapter establishes the scientific philosophy governing knowledge
discovery inside TSIS.

The objective of TSIS is not to search directly for profitable
strategies.

Its objective is to discover reproducible market phenomena that may
eventually support profitable strategies.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1.2 Representation versus Discovery

The Representation Architecture answers:

> What is observable?

The Phenomenon Discovery Architecture answers:

> What recurring phenomena can explain the observable market?

Representation organizes evidence.

Discovery generates hypotheses.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1.3 From States to Knowledge

The transition is:

``` text
Market States
        ↓
Research Question
        ↓
Hypothesis
        ↓
Empirical Evidence
        ↓
Phenomenon Candidate
        ↓
Scientific Validation
        ↓
Validated Knowledge
```

No phenomenon exists before evidence.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1.4 Research-first philosophy

TSIS does not assume that existing trading terminology is correct.

Concepts such as:

``` text
Absorption
Momentum
Liquidity Vacuum
Exhaustion
Price Discovery
```

are treated as hypotheses rather than truths.

The system must be capable of confirming, refining or rejecting them.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1.5 Phenomena are emergent

A phenomenon cannot be observed in a single trade or a single state.

It emerges from repeated evidence across many comparable states.

Therefore:

``` text
Single State
    ≠
Phenomenon
```

A phenomenon requires population-level evidence.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1.6 Scientific neutrality

The discovery process shall remain neutral with respect to:

-   trading style,
-   direction,
-   profitability,
-   discretionary terminology,
-   existing market beliefs.

Evidence precedes interpretation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1.7 Research populations

Every discovery process shall define its population explicitly.

Examples:

``` text
All Halt Events

All Scanner Candidates

All VWAP Reclaim Candidates

Matched Control Windows

Random Control Samples
```

Phenomena are always conditional on a declared population.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1.8 Evidence before explanation

The discovery process follows:

``` text
Observation
    ↓
Representation
    ↓
Evidence
    ↓
Explanation
```

Never:

``` text
Explanation
    ↓
Evidence
```

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1.9 Reproducibility

A discovered phenomenon must be reproducible by:

-   rebuilding the same states,
-   using the same populations,
-   applying the same methodology,
-   obtaining materially equivalent evidence.

Without reproducibility, no phenomenon becomes canonical knowledge.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1.10 Constitutional rule

Inside TSIS, phenomena are never assumed.

They are proposed, investigated, validated and continuously
re-evaluated.

Knowledge is therefore an evolving scientific product built upon
observable market representations rather than preconceived trading
beliefs.
